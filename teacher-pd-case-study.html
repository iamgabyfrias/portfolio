The problem

Theory that did not survive the classroom

High-school teachers with no technical background needed to teach cybersecurity, IT support, and networking, and teach it well. Most professional development hands teachers theory and hopes it holds up in front of students. These educators needed to feel the concepts in a real setting first, understand how the skills map to careers, and reach students in underserved communities while doing it.

The method

Experience first, theory second

Core move: industry-partnership learning

Through partnerships with Google, the Federal Reserve Bank of NY, and other organizations, teachers experienced the technical concepts in real workplace settings, then learned to adapt what they had seen for their own classrooms.

  1. Industry exposure

    Teachers learned from partners like Google and the Federal Reserve Bank of NY to see how the concepts show up in real work.

  2. Hands-on practice

    Participants worked through cybersecurity scenarios, IT troubleshooting, and networking labs to build the fundamentals.

  3. Curriculum adaptation

    Teachers translated those experiences into age-appropriate lessons for high schoolers.

  4. Classroom rehearsal

    Educators practiced facilitating with peer feedback, building confidence before teaching students.

The shift

Where teachers started, and finished

  • Hesitant about technical content
  • Unclear on real-world application
  • Unsure how to explain complex ideas
  • Limited sense of tech career paths
  • Worried about keeping students engaged

After

  • Teaching cybersecurity fundamentals with confidence
  • Connecting concepts to real careers
  • Making networking accessible

  • Adapting content for diverse learners

Results

A replicable model

Outcome

Over one month, 12 teachers with no technical background built the confidence and knowledge to teach the material well, and left with 30+ lesson plans in cybersecurity, IT, and networking they could actually use.

The model is replicable. It brings quality technical education to underserved schools without requiring specialized technical staff, by building teacher capacity instead.

What made it work

Lessons for adult learning

Experience before theory

Adults learn technical skills faster when they succeed first and understand the mechanics after.

Confidence is contagious

Confident teachers become better facilitators, which changes the room for students.

Peer learning works

Learning alongside colleagues created support that outlasted the workshop.

Scaffolding prevents overwhelm

Gradual complexity built durable skill instead of short-term cramming.
